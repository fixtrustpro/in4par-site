The story (GoFundMe page text)

In 1986, Harold Dunovant made sure Black golf would be remembered.

He saw that the players, builders and pioneers who carried Black golf through segregation were going unrecorded. So he started a record of his own: the National Black Golf Hall of Fame. Forty years later, that record holds more than 160 names, and it is still growing.

In [1963], Harold became the first Black golf professional to manage a golf course for the City of New York, at Kissena Park in Queens. He owned the golf shop and bought every piece of merchandise himself. One company would sell to him: Titleist.

Harold was the first Black graduate of the PGA Business School. He and his son Jeff are the only Black father and son to both be PGA members. Jeff carries his father's work forward today as General Manager of John A. White Golf Course in Atlanta.

Now we want to give Harold his due.

We are commissioning a bust of Harold Dunovant by [sculptor]. It will stand in the Hall of Fame's first permanent home, the new clubhouse at John A. White Golf Course, and be unveiled [at the clubhouse opening / at the 2027 induction] in front of the family, the inductees and the community he built.

It will be a companion to the bust honoring William Powell, who built Clearview Golf Club in 1946. The two founders belong side by side.

Our goal: 300 people at $100. Every $100 donor's name goes on the Founders Circle roll beside the bust.

"Titleist was the only company that gave my dad an opportunity to feed me, my mom, my brothers. Where would I be today? I don't think I would be playing golf without my dad. My dad showed me everything about the game and how to play the game."

Jeff Dunovant, Harold's son, PGA member and General Manager of John A. White Golf Course

How we'll know it's working

  • Each person sharing the campaign gets their own GoFundMe share link, which counts the gifts it brings in. Each channel and the PGA Show QR code also get a short link through blackgolfhof.org that counts clicks before sending people on to GoFundMe.
  • If the Hall claims the fundraiser as its nonprofit, GoFundMe lets it download each donation with its source.
  • A weekly tally every Monday: page visits, donors, dollars and the top channels. Half-weeks are scaled rather than read as full ones.
  • Every donor gets a thank-you within 24 hours and is added to the Hall's email list, so the 2027 induction starts with a warm list.
  • Targets to check against: 100 donors by the PGA Show, 150 by the end of February, $30,000 by July 4, and the bronze push in August.
